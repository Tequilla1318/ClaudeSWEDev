## Rejected alternatives

- **ATM from VIX through the smile, β = 0.22** (ATM/VIX = 0.908): PUT 2007–2016 +0.13 (pass); PUT 2017–now +1.13 (pass); PUTY 1990–2007 +2.08 (fail); PUTY 2008–now +0.87 (pass). Overstates PUTY before 2008.
- **Slope = 0.0059 x (SKEW − 100), ATM from VIX through the smile**: PUT 2007–2016 +1.76 (fail); PUT 2017–now +0.98 (pass); PUTY 1990–2007 +2.77 (fail); PUTY 2008–now +1.25 (pass). SKEW rose from about 116 before 2008 to about 140 since 2020, but the implied slope doesn't explain the VIX/ATM gap before 2008.

Both alternatives derive ATM vol from VIX through the smile. They fail because the VIX/ATM gap is not only skew: Carr & Wu (2006) attribute it mainly to Jensen's inequality and the variance of volatility. Keeping the separately validated 0.9 x VIX for ATM and adding a modest skew works.

Backtests of far-OTM rules therefore likely **understate** the premium sellers would collect today, and understate how much those puts reprice in a selloff.
